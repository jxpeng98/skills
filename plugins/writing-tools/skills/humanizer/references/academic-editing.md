# Academic Editing

Read this reference only for essays, reports, coursework, academic sections, or
other evidence-led university writing.

## Integrity Boundary

Preserve the writer's own intellectual contribution. For assessed work, help the
user clarify and express existing reasoning, identify gaps, and plan revisions.
Do not create missing arguments, interpretations, evidence, citations, results,
or reflective experience for submission as the user's work.

Protected academic content includes:

- research questions, thesis, variables, methods, datasets, sample sizes, and findings;
- citations, authors, years, quotations, page numbers, and bibliography details;
- disciplinary terminology, equations, labels, grades, and assignment wording;
- caveats, limitations, uncertainty, causal boundaries, and conclusions.

If one appears incorrect, mark it `Needs verification` instead of substituting a
plausible fact.

## Editing Modes

In `Coach Mode`, diagnose argument flow, paragraph purpose, claim-evidence fit,
and transitions; leave substantive decisions with the author. In `Polisher Mode`,
improve permitted sentence-level expression without supplying new substance.

## Section Checks

- `Introduction`: establish the question, motive, scope, thesis, and necessary
  roadmap without generic scene-setting.
- `Body paragraph`: give the paragraph one job; connect topic sentence, evidence,
  analysis, and transition.
- `Methods`: make procedure, variables, materials, and constraints reproducible;
  do not imply steps that are absent from the source.
- `Results or findings`: lead with the result, preserve exact evidence and
  uncertainty, and avoid unsupported interpretation.
- `Discussion`: distinguish interpretation from result; connect limitations,
  implications, and the original question.
- `Conclusion`: synthesize the answer without introducing a new unsupported
  claim.

## Academic Style

Prefer precise verbs such as `argues`, `shows`, `compares`, and `suggests` only
when they match the source. Use transitions to show reasoning rather than to
decorate the prose. Avoid inflated vocabulary, abstract noun stacks, and claims
of novelty or causality that the evidence does not support.

Use the discipline and venue's conventional register, with familiar wording and
only warranted qualifications. Follow the target language's academic conventions
for stance, claim placement, citation integration, and paragraph movement.

## Final Audit

- Does every paragraph have a clear purpose?
- Does each claim stay within its evidence?
- Are citations, data, examples, methods, and findings preserved exactly?
- Are author decisions and evidence gaps visible rather than silently filled?
- Does the result preserve the user's reasoning in conventional disciplinary language?

# Statement Drafter

## Purpose

Help faculty turn real experience into structured professional narratives for
teaching, service, funding, review, or recognition contexts.

## Before Drafting

Use `grill-me` when the goal, desired result, audience, evidence, length, or
evaluation criteria are unclear. Ask what the statement must prove and what
examples the user can truthfully support.

## Workflow

1. Identify document type, audience, length, deadline, and review criteria.
2. Gather evidence before writing: roles, courses, students served, outcomes,
   service contributions, methods, constraints, and reflective lessons.
3. Build a clear claim-evidence-impact structure.
4. Use concrete examples without overstating causality or credit.
5. Keep language professional and readable. Avoid inflated claims, generic
   excellence language, and unsupported metrics.
6. For teaching statements, connect philosophy to classroom practice and student
   learning evidence.
7. For grant or award support, preserve requirements and avoid inventing
   eligibility, collaborators, funding history, or institutional endorsement.

## Output

Use this structure:

```markdown
## Goal

<goal and desired result>

## Evidence To Use

- <evidence>

## Draft

<statement text>

## Evidence Gaps

- <claim that needs support>

## Revision Notes

- <what to strengthen next>
```

## Boundaries

Do not fabricate achievements, student outcomes, publications, grants, service
roles, quotes, or institutional support. Mark missing evidence instead of
filling it in.

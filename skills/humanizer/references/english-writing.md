# English writing

Follow the writer's regional English and the conventions of the setting. A
message to a colleague can sound warm and direct. A journal article often needs
a more precise account of who observed what. Preserve quotations, proper names
and terms of art in either setting.

## Give the sentence a verb

“The team reviewed the forms” works well when the reader needs to know who did
the work. “The forms were reviewed on Friday” can work just as well when the
forms and the date are the point. Keep words like *only*, *usually* and *may*
close to the phrase they qualify. When editing a translation, start with what
actually happens. Then choose the verb and preposition that go together in English.

## Carry the thought forward

Start the next sentence with something the reader recognises. Repeating the
name of a feature can help a reader who would otherwise have to guess what
“this” refers to. Give a concrete detail from the draft enough room to explain
the point. For instance, “Export downloads the filtered rows as a CSV file”
tells someone using the feature what they will get.

A conjunction earns its place through the relationship it expresses. “The
survey closed on Friday with 42 responses” brings a date and a count together.
“The form was shorter, but people still needed help completing it” has a real
turn in the thought. Keep that turn. If a report qualifies a measurement
several sentences later, move the qualification beside the measurement before
choosing a connector. Introduce a separate finding by naming it directly.
When the next paragraph takes up that finding, the shared subject can carry
the transition on its own.

## Let the occasion set the tone

Use contractions, first person and idioms when they belong to the writer's
voice. A routine notice can simply say “We've received your application. We'll
email you after the review.” If the original also asks applicants to keep their
reference number, keep that instruction where they can act on it. Read the
passage through for rhythm and check what the writer has actually promised.
